## 1. Bugs and reliability

### W-01 A restored tank's aquarium runs on `Math.random`, so `?seed=` cannot replay a saved tank

Size S · Severity high · Value 4/5 · Risk 1/5

**Problem.** The `?seed=` replay pin promises that two launches with the
same seed and the same tank behave the same. A fresh launch seeds the
aquarium's life-model stream deterministically, but a *restored* launch
throws that stream away and hands `Aquarium.fromJSON` a `Math.random`
closure. Every roll the life model makes on load — temperature-shock
disease, contagion spread, medicine cure, birth mutation — then diverges
between identical launches, which is exactly the class of event a seeded
replay exists to reproduce.

**Evidence.** `web/main.ts:264-265`
(`sim.aquarium = Aquarium.fromJSON(saved.aquarium, () => Math.random())`)
vs `core/sim.ts:444` (`this.aquarium = new Aquarium(makeRng(seed ^ 0x5eed))`)
and the documented pin at `web/main.ts:228-241`. The constructor's
derivation is `seed ^ 0x5eed`; `Aquarium.fromJSON(raw, rand)` takes the
stream as its second parameter (`core/aquarium/aquarium.ts:437`).
Reproduced by the helper: two `Aquarium.fromJSON(sameJson, () => Math.random())`
instances given 20 identical cold `changeWater` shock trials produced 7 vs
5 sick; two `new Aquarium(makeRng(7))` instances produced 20 vs 20.
`grep -n "Math.random" ANALYSIS.md` → no hits; B-51 and PRs #191/#197/#255
cover the seed and the pin itself, not this restore site.

**Change.** Give `Sim` a `restoreAquarium(raw: unknown)` that rebuilds the
aquarium from its own stream (`makeRng(this.seed ^ 0x5eed)`), storing
`seed` on the instance in the constructor, and call that from
`web/main.ts` instead of reaching for `Aquarium.fromJSON` there. Ambient
`Math.random` sites (paw visits, snail, plant bubbles, feed scatter) stay
as they are: they are outside the persisted life model and the code has no
documented ambient-stream boundary — note that in the entry rather than
quietly widening the contract.

**Acceptance.** Vitest in `core/sim.test.ts`: two `Sim`s restored from the
same save JSON through the new method, given the same 20 cold-shock trials,
produce identical sickness outcomes; the same two sims built through
`fromJSON(..., () => Math.random())` do not (pin the current divergence so
the test fails loudly if the fix is reverted).

**Merged and related.** Not filed. Adjacent: B-51 / PRs #191, #197, #255
(seed and replay pin); devin's audit notes ambient randomness under the
same heading.

### W-02 The auto-feeder runs on wall-clock uptime and ignores Aquarium Speed

Size S · Severity medium · Value 4/5 · Risk 2/5

**Problem.** The feeder fires every 45 minutes of *wall-clock* tank uptime
regardless of the aquarium's speed. At Time = 0 (fish frozen) it keeps
dropping pellets that then rot into the water; at Time = 10× the fish age
ten times as fast but are fed at the ordinary rate.

**Evidence.** `AUTOFEED_TICKS = 45 * 60 * TICKS_PER_SECOND` at
`web/main.ts:2302` with the comment "every 45 tank minutes" and "a paused
tank doesn't feed", checked as `sim.tickCount % AUTOFEED_TICKS` at
`web/main.ts:3809-3810` — `tickCount` is the fixed 30 tps wall clock
(`core/sim.ts`), not the speed-scaled aquarium clock. The aquarium's
`advance()` is what speed scales (`core/aquarium/aquarium.ts:105-109`),
and it early-returns at `speed <= 0`.

**Change.** As F-07's twenty-second-pass update already specifies: step the
next feeding in tank minutes inside `advanceLife`, gate on
`aquarium.speed > 0`, and read the original's `CODE` feeder routine before
deciding how catch-up meals apply.

**Acceptance.** Vitest: at `speed = 0` no pellets are dropped over a
simulated hour; at `speed = 10` the same number of feedings arrive in one
tenth of the wall time; at `speed = 1` today's 45-minute cadence is
unchanged.

**Merged and related.** **KNOWN: F-07** (its twenty-second-pass update
already says exactly this) and **KNOWN: G-01** in the fidelity report.
Open PR #228 (`feat/auto-feeder`) adds the timed feeder half — check it
before scoping.

### W-03 A dropped `.azpack` folder leaves no durable identity

Size M · Severity medium · Value 3/5 · Risk 2/5

**Problem.** Dropping an `.azpack` folder installs the art for the session
only: the spawned fish gets no pack binding, the backdrop is keyed under
the empty `src`, nothing is written to `installedAddons` or IndexedDB, and
there is no Overview row to remove it with. After relaunch the fish
reverts to the stand-in placeholder and the backdrop can be rebound by any
later `src:""` pick.

**Evidence.** `web/main.ts:3054-3068` calls `spawnFromDrop(idx,
pack.manifest.tag)` without the `pack`/`entry` arguments its signature
accepts (`web/main.ts:1077-1078`), and `pickBackdrop(imgs)` with the
default `src = ""` (`web/main.ts:1170`). Devin's read of the drop path end
to end.

**Change.** As already scoped: give the drop a synthetic `local:` source
URL, pass it to `pickBackdrop(imgs, src)` and `spawnFish(..., src, ...)`,
and record the install so the pack survives relaunch like a single-file
`local:` drop does.

**Acceptance.** Vitest over the drop handler: an `.azpack` drop writes an
`installedAddons` entry whose url is `local:`-keyed, the spawned fish
carries that url as `pack`, and a save→reload round trip restores the same
art instead of the stand-in.

**Merged and related.** **KNOWN: B-13** (remainder item 3).

### W-04 `feedFish` measures headroom against `FOOD_CAP` while the sim refuses at `MAX_UNEATEN`

Size S · Severity medium · Value 3/5 · Risk 1/5

**Problem.** The Feed command computes how much room there is with
`FOOD_CAP` (12) and the drop-time re-check uses the same number, but
`Sim.dropFood` refuses once 6 uneaten pellets are in the tank. With 6–11
pellets the pinch is scheduled, the pour sound plays, and no food enters
the water; with hints off there is no explanation.

**Evidence.** `web/main.ts:2511` and `:2528` vs `core/sim.ts:177` and
`:585`; `FOOD_CAP = 12` in `core/tuning.ts:26`, `MAX_UNEATEN = 6`. The
`noteFoodRefused()` hint is gated on hints being on (`web/main.ts:1025`).

**Change.** Count room and gate the pinch against `MAX_UNEATEN`, the sim's
real ceiling, and delete the dead `FOOD_CAP` constant if nothing else
reads it.

**Acceptance.** Vitest: with 6 uneaten pellets `feedFish` schedules no
pellets and plays no pour; with 5 it schedules at most one; the hint still
fires when hints are on.

**Merged and related.** **KNOWN: B-80** (PR #366) and now **PR #401**
(`fix/feed-cap-count`, open, from the twenty-third pass: `MAX_UNEATEN`
is aliased to `FOOD_CAP` at 12, `Sim.uneatenCount()` counts once, and the
manual feed, pinch re-check and auto-feeder gate all use it). Superseded
— do not open a competing PR; the 6-vs-12 framing above is what the code
did before #401.

### W-05 Bred fry never receive their parents' `entry`

Size S · Severity medium · Value 3/5 · Risk 1/5

**Problem.** `maybeBirth` copies `species`, `pack`, `sheetIdx` and the
rest onto the fry but not `entry`, so a fry born in a multi-`.fsh` add-on
is re-resolved through `sheetByPack` on the next relaunch and permanently
becomes a different species — even after the save-path fix lands.

**Evidence.** `core/sim.ts:1334-1342`. Reproduced against a bundled
engine: parents with `entry: "blackangel.fsh"` produced a fry with
`entry: undefined`.

**Change.** `entry: parent.entry` alongside `pack`/`sheetIdx`, with the
same `!== undefined` conditional spread the other copied fields use.

**Acceptance.** Vitest: a fry born to two fish carrying an `entry`
inherits it; a fry born to two fish without one gains no `entry` key.

**Merged and related.** **KNOWN: B-79** (PR #365 open) and the
twenty-third pass's **R-05** (same finding, filed at `57f9ae3`). Do not
open a competing PR.

### W-06 `Fish.entry` is dropped by every save and export

Size S · Severity high · Value 4/5 · Risk 1/5

**Problem.** `tankSnapshot()` serializes `sheetIdx`, `pack`, `name` and
`life` per fish but never `entry`, while `sanitizeSavedFish` reads it
back. On relaunch the survivors are re-bound by `legacyEntries`' round
robin over the add-on's entries, keyed by ascending id — release fish #3
while #4 stays, relaunch, and the remaining fish draws *and is named* as
its sibling forever. Export/Import Tank carries the same loss.

**Evidence.** `web/main.ts:461-469` vs `web/main.ts:384`, `:1283-1285`,
`:1386-1393`, `:1398-1402`; `web/tankmodel.ts`.

**Change.** Persist `entry` per fish (the filed change suggests extracting
a shared `savedFish()` helper so the write and read lists cannot drift
again).

**Acceptance.** Vitest: a save→`sanitizeSavedFish` round trip preserves
`entry`; a roster edit between save and reload leaves the survivor bound
to the entry it was created with.

**Merged and related.** **KNOWN: B-77** (PR #359 open). Do not open a
competing PR.

### W-07 Preferences is the only client window that does not fill its box

Size S · Severity medium · Value 3/5 · Risk 1/5

**Problem.** In a browser tab the client pages are `position: fixed; inset:
1px`, so the window is as tall as the viewport. Overview, Tank Stats and
Add-ons all stretch their content to match; Preferences keeps its panes at
the designed 383 px and pins the caption footer to the bottom, so every
pixel of extra height becomes dead gray between the two. At 1440×900 that
is a 414 px band across the full pane width — nearly half the window — and
on a 390×844 phone it is 358 px. The left pane-button strip ends after
Sound and leaves the rest of its 80 px column empty as well.

**Evidence.** Measured with `getBoundingClientRect` on the built `dist/`
at `f796688`:

| viewport | `#pfpanes` | visible `.pfpane` bottom | `#pffoot` top | dead band |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1440×900 | y22–893 | 417 | 831 | 414 px |
| 390×844 | y22–837 | 417 | 775 | 358 px |

The three siblings, same viewport 1440×900: Overview `#olist` y64–862 with
`#obar` y862–893; Stats `#stabs` y32–853 with `#sfoot` y853–893; Add-ons
`ilist`/`idetail` y62–843 with `#ifoot` y853–883 — all fill, no gap.
Relevant CSS: `.pfpane { position: absolute; left: 14px; right: 14px; top:
12px; }` (`web/app.css:213`), `#pfmachines`/`#pfpreview` fixed `height:
383px` (`:217`, `:224`), `#pffoot { bottom: 10px; height: 52px }` (`:300`).
`node_modules/osmium-ui/osmium.css:156` makes `.osm-page-window` fill the
viewport. Screenshot: `/tmp/finsical-mine/shots/prefs-tall.png`.

**Change.** Make Preferences behave like its three siblings: give
`.pfpane` the region between its top margin and the footer
(`top: 12px; bottom: 74px`), let `#pfmachines` and `#pfpreview` fill that
height instead of a fixed 383 px, and let the group-box panes centre their
contents vertically in the same region so they do not simply leave the
gray band below the last group. Keep `.pfpane[hidden] { display: none }`.
Do not change the designed behaviour at the native 565×518 window beyond
the panes using the space they already had.

**Acceptance.** Measured on the built page: at 1440×900 and at 390×844 the
gap between the lowest visible pane content and `#pffoot`'s separator is
under 40 px for every pane; at a 565×518 viewport every pane's content
still ends above the footer with no overlap and the machine list still
shows all 17 rows. A screenshot at 565×518 before and after differs only
in that region.

**Merged and related.** Adjacent **KNOWN: U-27** (fixed boxes *clip* when
the window is too small — this is the complementary case, when it is too

### W-14 Revert to… — dated autosaves in a Mac OS 8 File menu **(implement)**

Size M · Severity idea · Value 5/5 · Risk 2/5

**Problem.** The tank is written to `localStorage` every ten seconds and
every previous copy is thrown away, so a bad Remove, an Empty Tank or a
mis-poured water change is unrecoverable. Mac OS 8's File menu had
`Revert to…`, which listed dated autosaves in a sheet and threw away
everything after the one you picked. `U-04` records that Remove has no
undo.

**Evidence.** `web/main.ts:456-492` (`tankSnapshot`, save every 10 s),
`web/main.ts:152` (`parseTank`, the existing untrusted-input boundary),
`web/menubar.ts:392` (Window menu / `openClientWindow` pattern).

**Change.** Keep the last 20 snapshots in a ring buffered off
`tankSnapshot()`; a Tank/File `Revert to…` item shows them in an Osmium
alert sheet as *"Tuesday, 4:12 PM — 14 fish, water 78%"*; picking one
validates through `parseTank` before installing — never trust the buffer,
it is the same untrusted boundary as `localStorage`. Snapshots only while
the tank is owned, so B-34's lease still governs writes.

**Acceptance.** Vitest over the ring: it keeps 20 and drops the oldest; a
corrupt buffer entry is refused by `parseTank` and leaves the tank
untouched; a revert restores `tickCount`, the roster and the water to the
snapshot. A browser check: take a snapshot, Empty Tank, Revert, and the
fish are back.

**Merged and related.** Not filed. Distinct from F-26 (user-chosen named
tanks) and from an undo stack (`U-04`).

### W-15 The flyback transformer — a 15.7 kHz CRT whine you can switch on **(implement)**

Size S · Severity idea · Value 3/5 · Risk 1/5

**Problem.** Every CRT sang. A checkbox in the Monitor pane — **Flyback
whine (15.7 kHz)**, off by default, with a level slider — and Finsical
adds one detuned oscillator plus its 120 Hz harmonic at about
−42 dBFS: as loud as a real one, and audible mainly to people under about
35. It starts with the tube and stops when the tube sleeps, so it is the
one sound in the app that ever *stops*.

**Evidence.** `web/audio.ts` already owns `startAmbient`/`stopAmbient`,
the master gain and the mute path; `TankAudio.degauss()` is scoped at
`ANALYSIS.md:8175`; the CRT power state is `setCrt`/`CrtFilter` in
`web/crt.ts` and `web/main.ts`.

**Change.** `web/audio.ts` gains `startFlyback(level)` / `stopFlyback()`
beside the ambient pair, sharing the master gain and the mute path,
started only when `ctx.state === "running"` and the CRT reports powered
on. Two `OscillatorNode`s at 15734 Hz and 120 Hz, the fundamental at
`level * 0.006`, through the existing master gain with a 12 dB lowpass on
the fundamental so Safari does not clip. The checkbox and slider live in
the Monitor pane next to the CRT toggle; hidden entirely where there is no
monitor in the fiction (Linux and Android shells).

**Acceptance.** Vitest over the pure level mapping: `level 0` → gain 0,
`level 100` → the documented ceiling, mute → 0 regardless of level. A
browser check: with the CRT on and the box ticked the oscillator node
exists and is playing; unticking stops it; muting silences it; the CRT
powering off stops it.

**Merged and related.** Not filed. Distinct from D-06's degauss wobble and
its `degauss()` sound (a decaying transient, not a loop) and from D-37
(the volume slider).

**Problem.** Give every fish a resting spot — the point it hovers at
(`HOVER_ODDS` already picks one) — and leave a soft, slightly greasy
smudge on the inside of the glass there, drawn under the fish. Smudges
accumulate: a fish that has been in your tank for a week has left a dozen
overlapping prints around its favourite corner. Tap the glass and they do
not wipe off. The tank accumulates a history, which is the opposite of
what a screensaver-shaped app normally does.

**Evidence.** `core/sim.ts` hover branch (`HOVER_ODDS = 0.3`),
`web/water.ts` (`drawMurk`, `drawFood` — the layering order),
`web/snail.ts` (the sponge), `web/prefs.html:44` (the Effects pane, where
the Finsical-only extras live).

**Change.** The hover branch records `restX`/`restY`/`restAngle` and a
`restVisits` counter as runtime-only fields (like `hover` and
`hideTicks`, so they never reach the save). `web/water.ts` gains a
`smudges(fish[])` layer between `drawMurk` and `drawFood`, painting 2–3
offset translucent ellipses at the resting point oriented to the fish's
last `facing`, alpha `min(0.35, visits * 0.02)`, into an offscreen canvas
re-painted only when a count changes. The sponge clears them. Off by
default in the Effects pane.

**Acceptance.** Vitest over the pure layer: a fish at one resting point
for 50 ticks paints an alpha under the cap; two fish at the same point
accumulate rather than replace; the sponge resets every count to zero and
repaints empty. No new fields appear in `tankSnapshot()`.

**Merged and related.** Not filed. Distinct from D-12 (algae, a surface
growth with a wipe tool), D-28 (bubble trails) and V-22 (murk shimmer).
